1. Aggregate: Sand, gravel, or crushed stone mixed with cement to make mortar or concrete.
2. Butter: Applying mortar to the edge or surface of a brick or block before laying it.
3. Course: A continuous horizontal row of masonry units (like bricks or blocks).
4. Grout: A fluid mixture of cement, sand, and water poured into masonry voids to fill them.
5. Spread: Slang or shorthand for spreading mortar on a bed joint with a trowel.
6. Wythe: A continuous vertical section of a wall that is exactly one masonry unit thick.
7. Parge: Applying a thin coat of mortar or plaster to a masonry wall to seal or smooth it.
8. Lead: The corner or end of a wall built up first to guide the alignment of the rest.
9. Pointing: Filling and finishing the external mortar joints between bricks or blocks.
10. Temper/Tamper: Tempering is remixing mortar with water to restore workability; tampering (tamping) is compacting material.
11. Masonry cement: A pre-blended mix of Portland cement and plasticizers used to make mortar.
12. Lintel: A horizontal beam spanning an opening (like a window or door) to support the weight above.
13. Ranging: Lining up masonry units in a straight row using a line or level.
14. Closure: The final brick or block laid to complete a course.
15. Dry bond: Laying out masonry units without mortar to check the layout and spacing.
16. Rack back: Stepping back successive courses of masonry at a corner or end of a wall.
17. Mortar: A workable paste of cement, lime, sand, and water used to bind masonry units together.
18. Tongs: A scissor-like metal tool used to lift and carry multiple bricks at once.
19. Pavers: Flat, durable masonry units (often brick or concrete) used for walking surfaces or patios.
20. Corbel: A series of masonry units stepped outward from a wall to form a supporting ledge.
21. Rebar: Steel reinforcing bars placed inside masonry cores or concrete to add tensile strength.
22. Jamb: The vertical side of an opening, such as a window frame or doorway.
23. Toothing: Leaving alternating bricks projecting from a wall's end so a future wall can bond into it.
24. Bond beam: A horizontal reinforced concrete or block course designed to tie a wall together structurally.
25. Control joints: Intentional, continuous vertical joints built into a wall to prevent cracking from shrinkage or movement.
26. Flashing: Impermeable material placed in walls to redirect moisture to the outside.
27. Wash: A sloped top surface on a wall, sill, or ledge designed to shed rainwater.
28. Tread: The horizontal surface or step that you walk on in a staircase.
29. Riser: The vertical face or height between two consecutive steps in a staircase.
30. Sill: The horizontal masonry unit forming the bottom of a window or door opening.
